Evidence to design guidance

What the collected evidence suggests

DecisionObserved evidenceDesign implication
H1 routing-16.2% cost change; -5.24 s mean elapsed change across 156 same-settings, live-completed workflow pairs.Economic signal only. Check each case: lower cost does not always mean lower latency. No equivalent-quality or causal-savings conclusion.
H2 evaluator economics-98.9% cost change on 378 fully priced same-answer pairs.Decision evaluation has a strong observed token-cost advantage on this subset. Keep missing costs, unequal token caps and rate pacing visible.
H2 evaluator suitability90 disagreements / 450 paired judgments; negative agreement 13.5%; kappa 0.037.Do not deploy as an interchangeable sole quality gate on agreement evidence alone. Independently label discordant and defective examples before substitution.

High raw agreement can be dominated by both models accepting answers. Negative agreement measures consistency on rejection: twice the joint rejections divided by all rejection decisions. It is not defect recall. Low kappa can reflect class prevalence; neither this diagnostic nor raw agreement establishes which evaluator is right.

Optimize the entire workflow. Cheap fixed-choice routing is most useful when it does not add worker visits, repair loops or handoff friction. Evaluate cost and latency jointly, then inspect quality separately. Do not select only attractive cost dots.

Make evaluator substitution a separate decision. Inspect Decision-only accepts for potential missed defects and GPT-only accepts for possible unnecessary rejection, using independent scenario evidence. Neither direction identifies the correct model.

Do not tune the current experiment to agreement. Freeze these votes. A later fixed-choice-first policy with exception escalation must be tested end-to-end, including escalation and human correction cost.

Method and reproducibility

paired-hypotheses-v1. Case-stratified prompt-cluster bootstrap, seed 42, 2000 draws by default. Keep the two answers of each prompt together for H2. Intervals reflect the selected prompt mix; no multiplicity correction, independent reference labels, preregistered equivalence margin or calibrated evaluator reliability estimate. H1 workflows were not rerun by the separate evaluator repeatability study.

Same-settings H1 compares recorded worker model/settings, router settings and runtime version/build, excluding offline-revalidated arms. It is a sensitivity stratum, not randomized controlled evidence. All-selected measurements remain visible.

  • H1 and H2 are exploratory, not confirmed. No quality-equivalence margin or independent correctness labels were specified.
  • Selected recovery successes, runtime changes and nonconcurrent execution can confound routing comparisons, even within matched recorded settings.
  • One execution per prompt/arm: intervals describe variation across these prompts, not repeat-run reliability or a population sample.
  • Both evaluators are models, not ground truth. Agreement and consensus acceptance do not establish correctness or preserved quality.
  • H2 token caps differ (GPT 8192; Decision-1 2048). Both receive the same binary task, but this is not a controlled equal-budget comparison.
  • Fixed-study-v1 prices are study assumptions, not vendor invoices. Hosting, network and human checking/correction are unmeasured.
  • Unknown cost/time is never zero. Complete-cost comparisons exclude unpriced pairs and explicitly report their denominator.
  • All physical calls and internal repairs belong to their selected completed workflow/evaluation. Replaced workflows are separate archived spending.
  • Offline-revalidated answers retain original recorded elapsed time; recovery did not measure a new successful end-to-end execution.
  • Failure rates are operational diagnostics, not another hypothesis, a negative model vote or a coverage gate.
  • 95% prompt-cluster bootstrap intervals are exploratory, unadjusted for multiple comparisons; boundary/very small samples can give misleadingly narrow intervals.

Every input and generated artifact is SHA256-bound in manifest.json. Original answers, metrics, judgments and evaluator language are unchanged. This analysis performs no network, inference, deployment or evaluation.
